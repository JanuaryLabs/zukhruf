# A registered index keeps unit float32 rows under Venn's handle, and streamIndex gives a generator

Status: Proposed (2026-11-15).

## Context

Venn's `register_index(vectors, ids, outcomes)` (`venn/server.py:427-443`) checks 1 to 1,000,000 rows, divides each by its norm, builds a FAISS `IndexFlatIP` of them, and gives the handle `sha256(values.tobytes() + json.dumps([ids, outcomes]).encode())`. A state `{ index_ref }` of `decide` reads the rows (`venn/server.py:936`), a NearestReference question reads a labelled index (`venn/server.py:964-986`, `venn/index/decision.py`), and `stream_index` yields a line of NDJSON for each batch of an IndexApply question (`venn/server.py:869-910`). These facts of Venn's code decide how the package ports them:

- **FAISS.** No code of the library reads the `IndexFlatIP`: `decide`, NearestReference and `stream_index` read `index["vectors"]`. Only the adapters and the examples of Venn use FAISS, for their own stores.
- **The norm.** Venn makes a float32 unit vector in four places, with three algorithms. The bytes of the rows are part of the handle.

  | Venn's expression                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         | Port               | Pairwise sum              | float64 hypot, then fround | Sequential float32 sum | Nearest order found on arm64                 |
  | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------ | ------------------------- | -------------------------- | ---------------------- | -------------------------------------------- |
  | `values / np.linalg.norm(values, axis=1, keepdims=True)` (`register_index`, `venn/server.py:437`): NumPy's `add.reduce` along a row, its pairwise sum (`pairwise_sum`: one sum below 8 values, eight sums side by side up to 128, else two halves cut at a multiple of 8) | `Indexes`          | **100%** (6,000 of 6,000) | 72% to 84%                 | 7% to 21%              | the pairwise sum: **100%**                   |
  | `found[key] / max(np.linalg.norm(found[key]), 1e-12)` (`embed`, `venn/server.py:163`): a 1-D norm, `sqrt(x.dot(x))`, a BLAS `sdot`                                                                                                                                        | `vectorCache`      | 75.1%                     | 60.0%                      | 6.9%                   | 16 sums side by side, added in halves: 83.3% |
  | `values / np.linalg.norm(values)` (NearestReference, `venn/server.py:968`): a 1-D `sdot` too                                                                                                                                                                              | `NearestReference` | 79% to 82%                | 77% to 80%                 | 19% to 21%             | the same 16 sums: 87% to 90%                 |
  | `F.normalize(state_vector, dim=-1)` (`venn/server.py:1095`, `venn/index/apply.py:63`): PyTorch's CPU norm, which adds the float32 squares into the 8 lanes of a `Vectorized<float>`, then the lanes one after the other                                                   | `OneStateKind`     | 66% to 75%                | 66% to 71%                 | 18% to 21%             | those 8 lanes: **100%** (8,000 of 8,000)     |

  The probe of 2026-11-15 (NumPy 2.5.4 with Apple's Accelerate, PyTorch 2.15.0, on arm64) took 2,000 stored vectors (a float64 unit vector of 768 numbers rounded to float16, as Venn stores it), the 2,000 unit vectors that `embed` gives of them, and 2,000 given float32 states of sizes from 0.01 to 30. A rate is the share of vectors equal to Venn's bit for bit; each other differs by at most 3 units in the last place. No order of the probe gave Accelerate's `sdot` bit for bit: of 4, 8, 16, 32 and 64 sums, with or without a fused multiply-add, added in sequence, in halves or in pairs, 16 sums added in halves came nearest. No source states Accelerate's order, and NumPy on Linux uses OpenBLAS, whose `sdot` has its own. The 8 lanes of PyTorch are exact for the 768 numbers of the head on this CPU; for a width that is not a multiple of 8, PyTorch adds the rest in an order that the probe did not find. The lanes are those of `Vectorized<float>` for the CPU that PyTorch runs on: 8 with NEON or AVX2, 16 with AVX-512. The last column is the best order of the probe on one machine, not an order that Venn has on every platform.

- **The JSON.** `json.dumps` has `", "` between items and `ensure_ascii`: each UTF-16 code unit outside printable ASCII is `\uXXXX`. `JSON.stringify` writes them as they are.
- **The ids and the outcomes** are any JSON in Venn. Two ids are one when Python's `str()` writes them the same, so `1` and `"1"` are one. A NearestReference answer is `str()` of the outcome. A JSON number of JavaScript is one type, and Python reads `1` as an int and `1.0` as a float, whose `str()` and JSON differ.
- **Ties.** `np.argsort` sorts with an introsort that is not stable for large arrays, and by insertion, which is stable, for 16 values or fewer. NearestReference sorts the best scores of the outcomes and the scores of the rows with it.
- **The probability.** `torch.sigmoid(torch.tensor(float(margin) / t))` makes a float32 tensor of the float64 quotient, so the probability is a float32 number.
- **The stream.** `stream_index` checks everything before it returns its generator: the index, the `question_ref`, `row_cap` and `batch_size`, the threshold, then the question. It merges every key of the request over the registered question (`{**registered, **question}`), so a `family` of the request applies too, but the options stay those of the prepared question of the handle. `decide` checks `row_cap > 512` with a default of the count of the rows, so an index of more than 512 rows without a `row_cap` is refused with 413.
- **The response.** Venn's `decide` echoes `index_ref`, as it echoes `vector_tag` and `model`.

## Decision

1. `registerIndex(vectors, ids, outcomes?)` checks the rows with the checks of the given vectors of `decide`, with at most 1,000,000 rows, then the ids and the outcomes, in Venn's order. The rows are checked before they are copied to float32, so a count over the limit is refused without gigabytes of copies.
2. The index keeps the unit float32 rows, the ids and the outcomes, and no FAISS index. `index(handle)` gives them, with `outcomes` only for a labelled index: two types, `Index` and `LabelledIndex`, so NearestReference reads outcomes only where they exist.
3. Where Venn's order is NumPy's own, the port uses it: `Indexes` adds the float32 squares of each row with NumPy's pairwise sum, which is C code of NumPy, the same on every platform, through `ssumpw` of `@stdlib/blas-ext-base-ssumpw`, so the rows and the handle are Venn's bit for bit (100% of 6,000 rows). Where Venn's order belongs to BLAS (`sdot` in `vectorCache` and `NearestReference`) or to the vectorized kernel of PyTorch (`F.normalize` in `OneStateKind`), the order depends on the platform: Accelerate or OpenBLAS, and 8 or 16 lanes by CPU. There the port uses the correctly rounded norm, the float64 `Math.hypot` rounded to float32, which is the same on every platform, and does not copy the order of one machine. On the probe's machine it gives Venn's unit vector bit for bit for 60.0% of the stored vectors of `vectorCache`, 77% to 80% of the states of NearestReference and 66% to 71% of the states of `OneStateKind`; the orders of the last column did better there (83%, 87% to 90%, 100%), and would not on another platform. Each other vector differs by at most 3 units in the last place. The three places share `float32Norm` (`src/unit-vectors/float32-norm.ts`), and each keeps its own smallest norm: 1e-12 for `vectorCache` and `F.normalize`, none for NearestReference, whose state `Vectors` refuses at norm 0. A dot and a pairwise sum are other knowledge, so no module holds them together.
4. The handle is Venn's handle, bit for bit: the SHA-256 of the unit rows and of Python's `json.dumps([ids, outcomes])`, which `Indexes` writes for its arrays of strings and whole numbers: `", "` between items, and `ensure_ascii`. The text of a JSON value of the decision model (ADR 0005) is Python's `json.dumps` with `sort_keys` and without `ensure_ascii`, in `jsonText`. Each writes the part of `json.dumps` that its values need. With `pythonJsonDumps` of `@zukhruf/mteb` and `canonicalJson` of `@zukhruf/evals`, Python's `json.dumps` is written in four places, so it belongs in `@zukhruf/python` (backlog #4426), a change outside this package.
5. Ids and outcomes are strings or safe whole numbers (`Number.isSafeInteger`). A fraction, or a whole number from 2^53 up, is refused with `invalid_index`, where Venn takes it. The reason: the package cannot know if the caller meant Python's int or float, so neither the handle nor `str()` could be Venn's; and from 2^53 a number of JavaScript is no longer one int of Python. That rule is mine.
6. A state `{ indexRef }` is resolved before the other kinds, as in Venn. IndexApply gives each row the id of the index. Its `rowCap` defaults to the count of the rows, and more than 512 gives `stream_required` (413), from a default or a given `rowCap`.
7. NearestReference is a question kind of `decide`, with Venn's `nearest_decision` as its private member. It checks in Venn's order: the threshold (with every question), the index (`index_handle`), the outcomes and the state (`nearest_input`), two outcomes or more (`nearest_labels`), then `k` (`nearest_k`). The scores are computed in float64 from the float32 vectors and rounded to float32, the margin is rounded to float32, and the probability is the float32 sigmoid of the float32 quotient. With a threshold, a probability equal to it keeps the answer (`>=`, `venn/server.py:976`). `CalibrationKind` has `NearestReference`, the key that Venn reads.
8. The sorts are stable, in Venn's direction: ascending, then the last ones first. Of two outcomes or rows with one score, the later one comes first. For 16 values or fewer this is NumPy's order, and the tests pin it there. A larger tie has no defined order in NumPy, so the tests do not pin one.
9. `streamIndex(handle, question)` is `async` and gives a `Generator<IndexBatch>` of `{ offset, rows }`. It checks in Venn's order and embeds the texts of the question before it returns, so a refusal rejects the promise, as Venn raises when `stream_index` is called. An async generator would run no check before the first `next()`. The generator answers each batch when the caller reads it, as Venn's generator does. A `questionRef` takes each field of the request over the registered question, the `family` too, and the options and instructions of the registered question.
10. The decision has no `indexRef`, as it has no `vectorTag` and no `model` (ADR 0003): the caller gave it.
11. The branches of Venn for a family (a regress family, `family_state`, the head of a family) come with the families (U10, U11, U13).

## Consequences

- An index of the package and one of Venn with the same rows and ids have one handle. The golden tests compare the handles as strings, and the unit vectors of an index bit for bit.
- An index takes the memory of its float32 rows only: 3 GB for 1,000,000 rows of 768 numbers, and no copy for FAISS.
- The vector cache gives Venn's unit vector of a stored vector for 60% of stored vectors on the probe's machine, and the others differ in their last places; its tests keep their tolerance of 2 units in the last place. The handles and the unit rows of an index are Venn's bit for bit on every platform. The unit state before the logits is PyTorch's for 66% to 71% of states, and within a few units in the last place for the others, so the logits differ from Venn's by that and by the float32 of Venn's head against the float64 of the package.
- The answers of IndexApply and of `streamIndex` differ from Venn's by at most 4.7e-7 on the fixture, on rows near an option, whose probabilities are large. The probabilities and margins of NearestReference differ by at most 1.9e-8, within one unit in the last place of a float32 from 0.5 to 1. Both are float32 numbers, as Venn's are. The rounding of each score to float32 is not observable: Venn's scores come from a float32 matrix product of BLAS, whose order adds more than that rounding.
- The default `batchSize` of 65,536 is not pinned by a test: it needs 65,537 rows, 200 MB of fixture. A given `batchSize` of 65,536 is. The limit of 1,000,000 rows is tested without Venn, whose fixture would take 3 GB; Venn's check is the same line as for the 512 vectors of `decide`.
- A caller with ids that are fractions turns them into strings.
