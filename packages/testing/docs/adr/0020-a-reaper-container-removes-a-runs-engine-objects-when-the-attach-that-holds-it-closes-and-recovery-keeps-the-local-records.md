# A reaper container removes a run's engine objects when the attach that holds it closes, and recovery keeps the local records

Superseded in part on 2026-11-18 (#4508) by [ADR 0022](./0022-a-reaper-starts-again-after-a-restart-of-its-engine-and-removes-itself-at-its-end.md). The reaper has no `AutoRemove` (item 1). After its socket is verified, it gives itself the restart policy `unless-stopped`, and at its end it removes its own container. Thus the engine starts a reaper again after a restart of the engine, and it cleans its run with no later test run (item 9). A holder takes a lost hold again each `interval`, and waits while the reaper is `restarting` (item 7). A reaper that ended and stayed (item 7) is one whose self-removal failed or whose socket check failed (item 14): it now stays `exited` until a holder or recovery removes it. Recovery stays as the backstop until #4508 part 2 removes it.

Status: Accepted (2026-11-14, backlog #4205 with #2843). Amended on 2026-11-14 before the code: items 1, 4, 7, 9 and 10, and the new items 11 and 12, after the probe of several holders (item 7). Amended on 2026-11-15: items 1, 3 and 7, the hold, after a review found that a holder could come during the last pass or the expiry pass and leave its objects, and that a pass could remove what a live holder had just created. Amended again on 2026-11-15: items 6, 9 and 11, and the new item 13, the records, after a review found that the global teardown deleted a run's records before its reaper acted, so that no recovery reached a run whose reaper was then killed, and that a reaper left `created` with no next process of its run never ran. Amended on 2026-11-16: items 1 and 9, and the new item 14, the socket, after a run on socktainer showed that a reaper can mount a socket of another engine and remove nothing of its own run, with no error. It supersedes these parts of [ADR 0006](./0006-a-killed-run-is-cleaned-by-nodes-global-setup-and-teardown.md):

- the rejected option "Testcontainers' Ryuk";
- the consequence "The global teardown is synchronous ..." (the worker, `Atomics.wait`, 250 s and 240 s), for engine objects;
- the consequence about creating records and the 240 s recovery window;
- the engine part of "The global teardown removes what carries the run".

The rest of ADR 0006 stays: the run, its labels, the process records, the run's directory, recovery of local records, and the claim of a dead run by a rename.

## Context

### The cost today

The global teardown waits for its cleanup worker with `Atomics.wait` for at most 250 s (`CLEANUP_TIMEOUT` in `src/scripts/docker-global-setup.ts`). The worker gives each request to the engine 240 s (`ENGINE_CALL_TIMEOUT` in `src/scripts/run-cleanup.ts`). `Atomics.wait` takes only real time, and `AbortSignal.timeout` does not read the clock input. Thus the tests of these two budgets wait the real time. In the #3737 replica run (4 CPUs, `--parallel=3`, 2026-11-09):

| Test                                                                                                                                                             | File                                                          | Time  |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- | ----- |
| a global teardown whose cleanup takes longer than 250 s keeps the run's records and sets exit code 1                                                             | `src/scripts/docker-global-setup-timeout.integration.test.ts` | 251 s |
| a recovery whose drop of a dead run's database never gets an answer from its server ends within the engine call timeout, keeps that database and removes the run | `src/scripts/database-cleanup.integration.test.ts`            | 244 s |

`testing:test` is the longest task on the CI critical path. A test-only option is not allowed. The maintainer asked a different question on 2026-11-14: "can we eliminate the whole point of setting timeout and make it natural?". The ruling was to follow the leading tools: testcontainers' Ryuk removes the resources after the test process disconnects, so no test process waits.

### Why ADR 0006 rejected Ryuk

ADR 0006 says:

> Ryuk removes Docker objects only. It does not remove the host directories on a remote engine or the SSH forward directories. It also needs a container with the engine's socket mounted, which a shared server must allow.

The first two sentences stay true. This ADR answers them with a split: the reaper removes the engine objects, and recovery keeps the local records (see Decision 6). The third sentence had no evidence. The probe below tested it.

### The probe of 2026-11-14

The probe is in `~/.claude/scratch/4205/` (`fact-a.sh`, `client.ts`, `harness.ts`, `run-all.sh`, `logs/`).

- **Fact (a): an engine allows a container with its socket mounted.** A container with `/var/run/docker.sock` mounted created, listed and removed labelled containers and volumes on Docker Desktop (engine 29.8.0) and on the SSH engine `limerence-dokploy` (engine 28.5.0, with AppArmor, seccomp, a cgroup namespace and swarm active). Neither engine refused it.
- **Fact (b): a reaper held by a stdin attach removes the run's objects after `SIGKILL`.** The client created the reaper as `EngineLease` creates a lease (`OpenStdin`, `StdinOnce`, `AttachStdin`, attach with `ref: false`). It wrote one filter line, created labelled objects, and was killed with `SIGKILL`. The reaper read the end of its input, waited 2 s, did one pass (`docker rm -f -v` by the filter, then `docker volume rm -f`), and did 5 more passes 1 s apart.

| Engine                          | Cases                                               | Runs | Objects left after 15 s | Removal after the kill | Reaper exit |
| ------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- | ---- | ----------------------- | ---------------------- | ----------- |
| Docker Desktop 29.8.0           | settled, create in flight at once, at a random time | 15   | 0                       | 2.12 s to 2.15 s       | 0           |
| `limerence-dokploy` 28.5.0, SSH | settled, create in flight at once, at a random time | 15   | 0                       | 2.26 s to 3.93 s       | 0           |

The SSH times include the poll of the harness over SSH. Creates that were in flight at the kill were removed too: the engine finished them before the first pass.

## Decision

1. **The reaper.** A reaper is a container with a socket of the engine's host mounted at `/var/run/docker.sock`, as a `bind` mount. Before it lets a holder in, it makes sure that this socket goes to its own engine (item 14). A holder process holds it with one exec of `cat` on the standard input of the exec, with `ref: false` (`Container.spawn` of `@zukhruf/docker`). When the holder exits for any reason, also on `SIGKILL`, its connection closes, the engine ends the input of that `cat`, and the `cat` exits. The main process of the reaper counts its `cat` processes. When none is left, it closes its door (item 7), removes what matches its filter, and exits. The filter is in its environment (item 4). The reaper has `AutoRemove`, so the engine removes it after it exits. The exec is the session, not a TCP port: the port of Ryuk must be reachable from the client, and on an SSH engine it is not. The exec goes through the same transport as each other engine request.
2. **What the reaper removes.** It removes the containers with the run's label, with force and with their anonymous volumes, and then the volumes with the run's label. It never removes a container with the shared-server label (ADR 0006 and [ADR 0019](./0019-a-shared-servers-name-comes-from-its-request-and-the-global-teardown-removes-it-after-a-ttl.md)). Item 8 says how the reaper drops the run's databases inside shared servers.
3. **The grace and the re-check passes.** The engine can finish a create after the process that sent it died. Thus the reaper waits a grace after its last holder ended, and then does its first pass. After the first pass it does more passes at an interval, as Ryuk does. A pass that finds an object removes it. A holder that comes during the grace stops the countdown, as a client that connects again stops Ryuk's, and the reaper counts again. From the first pass on, the door of the reaper is closed (item 7): a holder that comes then is refused and holds the next reaper of the run. The re-check passes stay, for the creates that a dead holder still had in flight.
4. **The reaper's timeouts are its own configuration.** The grace, the re-check interval, the number of re-check passes, the time limit of each request to the engine, the number of retries, the wait for the first holder, the TTL of a shared server, and the wait, tries and patience of a sweep lease (ADR 0014) are inputs of the reaper, in the environment of its container, as Ryuk reads `RYUK_*`. The testbed gives the defaults. The first defaults are the values of the probe and of Ryuk: a grace of 2 s, 5 re-check passes 1 s apart, 10 s for each request and 10 retries; 60 s for the first holder, as Ryuk waits 60 s when no client ever connected; and the values of ADR 0014 and ADR 0019: a TTL of 7 days, a sweep-lease wait of 30 s, 3 tries and a patience of 5 s. The TTL stays a constant of the package's API (ADR 0019 item 6); only the reaper takes it as an input. The tests of the reaper start a reaper with short values (tens of milliseconds), as the tests of Ryuk do. This is not a test-only option of a test process: the reaper is its own unit, and these are its inputs. A removal that fails is tried again one re-check interval later.
5. **What goes away in the test process.**
   - The global teardown no longer waits for the engine. The worker, `Atomics.wait` and `CLEANUP_TIMEOUT` (250 s) go away for the engine objects.
   - The test-process side of `ENGINE_CALL_TIMEOUT` (240 s) goes away: the cleanup of the run's objects, `connectAndCleanup`, and the 240 s window of creating records. The re-check passes of the reaper do the job of the creating records. Recovery still connects for a dead run that has process records (item 9).
   - The two tests in the table above become tests of the reaper with short timeouts: "a reaper whose removal takes longer than its request time limit retries and exits", and "a reaper whose drop of a database never gets an answer ends within its request time limit and keeps that database".
6. **Local records stay with recovery.** The reaper runs on the engine's host. It cannot reach the SSH forward directories or the temporary directories on the machine of the test process. These records stay in the run's directory. The global teardown removes them with synchronous file system calls, which need no worker. Recovery of a later run removes what a killed run left, as ADR 0006 says.
   - **What the teardown keeps (amended on 2026-11-15, the records).** The global teardown removes each recorded path and then its record. It deletes the run's directory only when the run has no process record: then no process of the run reached the engine. Otherwise it keeps `run.json` and the process records, also when each process held the reaper. The teardown cannot know whether the reaper has acted, because it makes no engine call (item 5), and a reaper can still be killed in its grace or its passes: at a restart of the engine, by an out-of-memory kill, or by `docker rm -f`. The kept records are what recovery needs to reach that run (item 9). Before this amendment, the teardown deleted the records of a run whose processes held the reaper, and a reaper record (`reaper-<pid>.json`) told it so. That record is gone: recovery now asks the engine.

7. **One reaper for each run.** The first test process that uses Docker creates the run's reaper on its first use, on each engine. Each test process holds that reaper with its own `cat` exec. The reaper acts when the last `cat` ends. A run that never uses Docker makes no engine call, so ADR 0012 and ADR 0014 stay true. The filter is the run's label. testcontainers-go has the same shape: one Ryuk for each test session, started with the first container, which counts its connections.
   - **Why an exec, not the attach of item 1 before this amendment.** A probe on 2026-11-14 (`~/.claude/scratch/4205/impl/probe/multi-attach.ts`, Docker Desktop 29.8.0) attached two processes to the standard input of one container and killed them one at a time with `SIGKILL`. With `StdinOnce: true`, the main process read the end of its input at the death of the first holder, while the second one still lived. With `StdinOnce: false`, it never read the end, also after both died. The engine thus gives one container no way to see the last of several attaches close.
   - **The probe of the counting reaper** (`~/.claude/scratch/4205/impl/counting/`): 2 and 3 holder processes on one reaper, 2 runs each, on Docker Desktop 29.8.0 and on `limerence-dokploy` (28.5.0, over SSH). Each holder created 2 containers and a volume, and the holders were killed with `SIGKILL` one at a time, 9 s apart. After each kill but the last, each object stayed and the reaper ran. After the last kill, the reaper removed each object in 2.30 s to 2.43 s on Docker Desktop and in 4.42 s to 6.04 s over SSH (with the poll of the harness over SSH), and exited.
   - **The door (amended on 2026-11-15).** Ryuk registers each client synchronously, on an unbuffered channel, "so that we prevent the race on connection count", and answers it with an `ACK`. When `pruneWait` decides to prune, `defer r.shutdownListener()` closes its listener, and a client that comes after that is closed with no `ACK`, "which should trigger the caller to retry and get a new reaper" ([`reaper.go`](https://github.com/testcontainers/moby-ryuk/blob/main/reaper.go), `pruneWait`, the accept loop and `shutdownListener`). The reaper does the same with a lock file, its door, and BusyBox `flock`:
     - **The hold is its ACK.** The `cat` exec of a holder takes a shared lock on the door with `flock -n -s`, prints `held`, and then runs `exec cat`, which keeps the lock as long as the `cat` lives. A hold is taken only when the holder read `held`. A holder whose lock is refused prints `closing` and ends: that reaper is closing. The holder then waits until the engine removed it, and holds the next one.
     - **Closing the door is atomic.** After the grace with no holder, the reaper takes the exclusive lock with `flock -n -x`. When that fails, a holder came, and the reaper waits again. It keeps the lock through each pass, the expiry pass and its exit, so no live holder creates during a pass or after the last count. The re-check passes stay, for the creates that a dead holder still had in flight. Ryuk also keeps what was created after its prune decision ("changes detected"); the reaper does not, because with its door closed only a dead holder's create can come after that decision. The probe of 2026-11-15 in `docker:28.5.2-cli`: the `cat` inherits the descriptor of the lock; the exclusive lock is refused while a shared one is held; a shared lock is refused while the exclusive one is held.
     - **The count stays `pgrep -x cat`.** The reaper waits for the end of its holders by counting their `cat` processes, and uses the lock only to close. A try of the exclusive lock to count would refuse a holder that comes in that moment, and that holder would wait for a reaper that does not close.
   - **A hold has the reaper's limits.** A holder waits for the removal of a reaper that is closing for at most the time that reaper can still run: its passes with each removal retried, and its expiry pass with one sweep (`closingTime` in `src/docker/reaper.ts`). It tries again at most the number of retries (10) more times. Then the create fails with an error that names the reaper. The readiness bound of the hold (item 15, amended on 2026-11-17) ends these waits first when they take longer.
   - **A hold that ends while its process lives is taken again at once**, not on the next create: on the same reaper when the cut was shorter than the grace, as after a short break of an SSH connection, and else on the next one. The waits of that hold keep no process running. A create, and each `defaults()`, waits for that hold. Thus a process that took the labels from `defaults()` once and creates with its own client stays held.
   - **A reaper that ended and stayed is removed by the holder** (with `force`), and so does `startReaper`, instead of a wait for `AutoRemove` that never comes.
8. **Databases inside shared servers.** The reaper drops the run's databases by an exec into each shared server, with the server's own client (`psql`, `mysql`, `sqlcmd`, with the credentials in `Config.Env`). Thus the teardown makes no engine call. A probe proves the clients in each image and the time of an exec on a loaded server before code.
   - The probe of 2026-11-14 (`~/.claude/scratch/4205/impl/probe/drop-by-exec.sh`) dropped a test database by an exec of the image's own client in `postgres:18-alpine` (`psql`, 0.04 s), `mysql:lts` (`mysql`, 0.04 s), `mariadb:lts` (`mariadb`, 0.05 s) and `mcr.microsoft.com/mssql/server:2022-latest` (`/opt/mssql-tools18/bin/sqlcmd`, 0.21 s). The `mcr.microsoft.com/azure-sql-edge` image has no `sqlcmd`, `bcp`, `python3`, `perl` or `curl`: each exec ends with exit code 127.
   - Thus `SqlServer` runs the full image on every engine, and Azure SQL Edge goes, with the export `SQL_SERVER_EDGE_IMAGE` (the maintainer, 2026-11-14). Microsoft retired Azure SQL Edge on 30 September 2025, and it no longer supports ARM64 ([What is Azure SQL Edge?](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/azure-sql-edge/overview), [retirement notice](https://azure.microsoft.com/updates/v2/azure-sql-edge-retirement)). The registry gives `mssql/server` (`2022-latest`, `2025-latest`) for `linux/amd64` only. Microsoft does not test or support it under emulation such as Rosetta 2 or QEMU ([the Docker quickstart](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/sql/linux/quickstart-install-connect-docker)). On Docker Desktop 29.8.0 (aarch64), the emulated image answered `SELECT 1` 5.8 s after `docker run` in each of 3 runs, and the four SQL Server fixture tests of this package passed. The MSSQL module of testcontainers-node tests only `mcr.microsoft.com/mssql/server` images ([`mssqlserver-container.test.ts`](https://github.com/testcontainers/testcontainers-node/blob/main/packages/modules/mssqlserver/src/mssqlserver-container.test.ts)).
9. **The TTL sweep of shared servers.** `removeExpiredServers` (ADR 0019, 7 days) moves into the reaper. The reaper does it after the pass of its run. Recovery keeps its sweep by the run's label for a run whose reaper is gone, as a backstop for a reaper that was killed (for example at a restart of the engine). Recovery does this sweep with a reaper too: it asks the reaper of the dead run for a hold, as a test process does, and ends that hold when it gets `held` (`startReaper` in `src/docker/reaper.ts`). It does not wait for the passes of the reaper. The reaper then closes after its grace and does its passes.
   - **A holder that came and left counts (amended on 2026-11-16, the mark).** The hold of recovery can end before the reaper looks for its first holder. The reaper counts its `cat` processes, so it did not see that hold, and it waited its full wait for a first holder (60 s). So each holder writes a mark in the reaper (`/tmp/came`) when it holds the door, before it prints `held`. The wait for a first holder ends at a `cat` or at that mark. The door does not change: the reaper still closes it only after its grace with no `cat`, and a holder that holds the door shared still makes the close fail. Recovery does not start the reaper with no wait for a first holder (`firstHolder` 0): with a short grace, a reaper can finish before the hold asks it, and recovery cannot tell a reaper that finished from one whose check failed.
   - **Why a hold, not a start (amended on 2026-11-16, the socket).** Before this amendment, recovery started the reaper with no wait for a first holder (`firstHolder` 0) and did not ask it. Since item 14, a reaper ends also when its socket fails the check. The engine shows the same thing for the two cases: a reaper that ended and was removed. Only the answer of a hold tells recovery that the check passed, and when it failed, the hold tries the next socket.
   - **The hold of recovery keeps the process running (amended on 2026-11-17, the wait).** The exec of a hold has `ref: false`, so it keeps no process running. While the reaper does its check, nothing else did, and the global setup ended before the hold had its answer ("Detected unsettled top-level await"). Thus recovery waits for its hold as a test process does (`ReaperHold.held`): with a timer that keeps the process running, for at most the readiness bound of the hold (item 15, amended on 2026-11-17). A hold that takes longer fails with an error that names the reaper and that bound.
   - When the reaper of that run still exists, recovery leaves the run to it. Ryuk has no backstop. The maintainers chose to keep one.
   - **When recovery deletes the records (amended on 2026-11-15, the records).** The engine decides, not the records. The owner reference of ADR 0011 makes each object of the run findable by the run's id, so recovery asks the engine whether the run still owns something. For a dead run with a process record, recovery:
     1. looks for the reaper of the run by its name. When it exists, recovery calls `startReaper`, which leaves a running reaper alone, starts a `created` one, and replaces one that ended and stayed. Recovery keeps the records.
     2. else lists what the reaper of the run removes (item 2 and item 8): the containers with the run's label but no shared-server label, the volumes with the run's label, and the databases whose name matches the run's id (ADR 0011 item 4) in each running shared server. When it finds one, a reaper was killed before it removed it: recovery starts a new reaper with `startReaper` and keeps the records.
     3. else deletes the records: no reaper of the run is left, and nothing that one would remove.
   - A run's records are thus deleted by the first recovery after its reaper ended with nothing left. A reaper that finished leaves nothing, so its run's records go at once and recovery touches nothing on the engine. A reaper that was killed leaves what it had not removed, and the next recovery starts a new one; the recovery after that one ended deletes the records.
   - A shared server that does not run or does not answer its list within one request time limit of the reaper (`requestTimeout`, 10 s) counts as done, as in ADR 0011 item 6. Each recovery lists the databases of each server once, for all its dead runs.
   - **The bound.** The records of a run stay while its reaper exists, or while the engine holds an object of the run. A reaper lives at most its wait for a first holder, its grace and its closing time (`closingTime` in `src/docker/reaper.ts`) after its last holder ended. An object of a run with no reaper gets one at the next recovery. Thus the records that pile up on an engine are those of the runs whose reaper still ran at the last recovery, which ended within one reaper lifetime before it (about 7 s with the defaults: a grace of 2 s and 5 passes 1 s apart, and at most the closing time), and those of the runs whose objects each reaper failed to remove. The second kind is the backstop doing its job: the records stay exactly as long as the objects. The runs of another engine stay as ADR 0006 says, until a run on that engine recovers them.
   - The cost: the global setup that follows a run that used Docker connects to the engine, inspects the reaper of that run, lists the containers and volumes of the run, and runs one list in each shared server. A run that never used Docker still makes no engine call, and neither does the global setup after it (ADR 0012).
   - Kubernetes deletes an owner with the `foreground` propagation policy the same way: the owner stays, with a `foregroundDeletion` finalizer, until the garbage collector removed each dependent that blocks it ([Garbage collection](https://kubernetes.io/docs/concepts/architecture/garbage-collection/#foreground-deletion)). The records are the owner, and the engine's objects are its dependents.
10. **The image of the reaper.** The reaper uses an official `docker` CLI image, pinned by version (`docker:28.5.2-cli`; the first probe used `docker:27-cli`), with the script in the command. The package builds no image. The sweep lease of item 9 is the `cat` container of ADR 0014 (`busybox:1.37.0`), held by a `docker run -i` of the reaper.
11. **`defaults()` holds the reaper before it gives the labels.** `DockerTestbed.defaults` was a synchronous getter. It becomes `async defaults()`: in a run, it holds the run's reaper on the testbed's engine, by the same path as a create, and then gives `{ Labels, HostConfig }`. Outside a run, it gives the labels with no engine call. There is no synchronous variant. Thus another Docker client that creates with the run's labels (a sandbox launcher, the child processes of the lock-store matrix of the mutex) leaves nothing behind after the run (the maintainer, 2026-11-15).

- The leaders do the same. testcontainers-node gives the session label only through `await getReaper(client)`: its synchronous `createLabels()` has no session id, and `GenericContainer.start()` sets `Labels[session-id] = reaper.sessionId` after `const reaper = await getReaper(client)` (`packages/testcontainers/src/reaper/reaper.ts`, `src/generic-container/generic-container.ts`). In testcontainers-java, `DockerClientFactory.instance().client()` runs `ResourceReaper.instance().init()`. testcontainers-go's synchronous `GenericLabels()` starts no reaper, the shape this item rejects.
- Not a background hold started by a synchronous getter: the other client's create can reach the engine before the hold, and then the reaper can end before it is held, and the error of a failed hold reaches no caller.
- A run whose recorded processes held no reaper (for example one whose processes only made a host directory) keeps its records at the global teardown, as each run with a process record does (item 6, amended on 2026-11-15). The next run's recovery finds no reaper and nothing of the run on the engine, and deletes the records (item 9).

12. **One drop for each kind.** Each kind gives its client command and its SQL once. Disposal runs them with an exec in the server's container, and the reaper's script runs the same command and SQL with `docker exec`. SQL Server drops by `sqlcmd` too now, and the collectors of ADR 0011 lose `open` and `list`.

13. **A reaper left `created` is started by the next reaper on its engine (added on 2026-11-15).** A process killed between the create and the start of its reaper leaves it `created`. A `created` container never runs, so `AutoRemove` never removes it, and nothing of its run is removed. The next process of that run starts it (item 7, `running` in `src/docker/reaper.ts`), and so does recovery when the run has records (item 9). A run with no next process and a run with no global setup (a test that sets `ZUKHRUF_TESTING_RUN_ID` itself) have neither. Thus each reaper, after its expiry pass and with its door still closed, lists the reapers of other runs that are `created`, and starts each one whose age is more than twice the `requestTimeout` in that reaper's own environment: its creator sends one inspect and one start after the create, each within that time. A `docker start` is safe in a race: the creator's own start of the same reaper also succeeds, and the reaper then runs once. A started reaper waits for a first holder (item 4, 60 s by default), and then does its passes. A reaper that is not `created` is never touched: a running one has holders or will close by itself.

- The reaper is the home, not recovery. Recovery runs only in a global setup, sees only the runs of its own user and engine endpoint, and would have to call the engine at each global setup to look, also after a run that never used Docker (ADR 0012). The reaper already sweeps the whole engine for expired shared servers (item 9), runs on each engine that a run used, and reaches the reapers of fake runs, of other machines and of other users.
- A sweep lease (ADR 0014 item 5) is removed when it stays `created`, because its creator removes servers once it holds it. A reaper is started instead: a started reaper removes only the objects of its own run, after its holders end, so a slow creator that is still alive holds it as usual.

14. **The reaper makes sure that its socket goes to its own engine (added on 2026-11-16).** The path of the engine's socket on the engine's host is not always `/var/run/docker.sock`, and that path can go to another engine. On this Mac, `/var/run/docker.sock` is a link to the socket of Docker Desktop. A reaper on socktainer (Apple's engine) mounted it, removed nothing of its run on Apple's engine, and gave no error (#4205, the note of 2026-11-15 from #4430).

- **The check.** Each reaper has a label with a random value of its own. Before it lets a holder in, the reaper lists the containers in any state with that label through its socket (`docker ps -a`: the engine marks a container running only after its process started, so a list of the running containers can miss a reaper in its first moments), and writes the result to `/tmp/bind`. The result is `verified` when it finds itself. Else it is the reason: the socket does not answer, or it goes to an engine that does not run this reaper. The `cat` exec of a holder waits for that file. It prints `held` or `closing` (item 7) only after `verified`, and else `unverified` and the reason. A reaper whose check failed removes nothing. It waits for a holder to remove it, and ends by itself after its wait for a first holder and one request timeout.
- **The candidates.** A hold tries these sources of the socket on the engine's host, in this order, and keeps the first one that is verified: `/var/run/docker.sock`, and then the path of the client's socket when the client goes to the engine through a `unix://` host. The mount is a `bind` mount (`HostConfig.Mounts`), not a `Binds` entry: a source that the engine's host does not have fails the create, and the engine creates no directory there. The hold removes a reaper whose check failed, and tries the next source. When no source is verified, the hold fails with an error that names the engine and each source with its result. The reaper has the source of its socket in a label, and the next hold of the process tries the source of the last reaper that answered first.
- **The bound of the candidates (amended on 2026-11-17).** A reaper of the run that another process created can have another source than the one the hold asked for. Thus a failed check marks both sources as tried: the one the hold asked for, and the one of the reaper that answered. Each try marks one more source, so a hold asks at most one reaper for each source (two at most), and then fails with the error that names each source. All the tries are inside the readiness bound of the hold (item 15).
- **No leading tool checks its socket.** testcontainers-node and testcontainers-go guess the source: `/var/run/docker.sock` for Docker Desktop, else the path of the client's socket, and `TESTCONTAINERS_DOCKER_SOCKET_OVERRIDE` when the user sets it. A socket that goes to another engine thus removes nothing, with no error. This design is ours. It has no override variable and no rule for an engine by name: the check finds the right socket, or fails and says why.
- **The probe of 2026-11-16** (the mac-mini engine, Docker Desktop 29.9.2, over SSH; `self-check-probe.sh` in the scratchpad of the main session, with the results on #4205). A `docker:28.5.2-cli` container looked for its own label through its socket. With `/var/run/docker.sock` mounted, it found itself. With the macOS path of the client's socket mounted, it found itself. With the socket of a docker-in-docker engine mounted through a volume, the socket answered, but the container was not on that engine. A `bind` mount of a source that does not exist failed with "bind source path does not exist" and created nothing.

15. **A hold has a readiness bound (added on 2026-11-17).** A hold is a readiness step, as the start of a shared server is. Its readiness bound is 60 s (`readiness` in `REAPER_DEFAULTS`, amended on 2026-11-17 from 120 s), the connection time limit of Ryuk in Testcontainers (1 minute). It covers the whole hold: the pull of the reaper's image, the create, the start, the check of the socket (item 14) and the answer `held`. At the bound, each call to the engine of the hold stops, and the hold fails with "The hold of the reaper … was not ready within its readiness bound of … ms". A process that waits for its hold keeps running for at most this bound (item 9). A hold that is taken again while its process lives (item 7) has the same bound, but its timer keeps no process running. Like the other settings of the reaper (item 4), `readiness` is an input, and the tests set it short, as the tests of Ryuk set its time limits to 50 to 500 ms. This is not a test-only option.

- **Why.** The time limits on the path of a test must nest. The first test of a process can pay the hold and then the start of a server, so the time limit of a test is the sum of these readiness bounds plus 60 s for the test: 60 s + 180 s (SQL Server) + 60 s = 300 s (the README; `@zukhruf/nx` ADR 0004). Before, the bound of a hold was a product of worst cases: each try (the retries plus one, 11) times one request time limit and the closing time of a reaper, about 9.4 h with the defaults. The pull of the image had no bound. A test that waited for a hold with a shorter limit of its own (10 s) caught a slow hold, not only a hold that never comes (#4205, CI run 44065152278).
- **The closing time stays.** A hold still waits for a reaper that is closing for at most its closing time (item 7). The readiness bound ends that wait first when it is longer.

## Not covered by the probe

- A create that the engine finishes after the first pass. In 30 runs, the re-check passes never removed an object.
- Networks and images. The testbed does not create them now.
- Rootless engines. Their socket is not `/var/run/docker.sock`. The client's `unix://` path of item 14 goes to one only when the client runs on the engine's host.
- A machine that goes away without closing its connection. The engine sees the end of the attach only when the TCP connection ends. On an SSH engine, `ClientAliveInterval` of `sshd` is 0 by default, and TCP keep-alive takes about 2 h (#2843).
- A holder that is killed between the create and the start of its reaper. The reaper stays `created` and never runs. The process created nothing yet, so only the reaper stays, and the next process of the run starts it, or, when the run has no next process, the next reaper of another run on that engine (item 13). ADR 0014 item 5 has the same case for a sweep lease. A holder killed between the start and its `cat` leaves a reaper that does its passes after the wait for a first holder.
- BigQuery datasets (#2843). They are not engine objects, and a reaper needs credentials for them.

## Considered Options

- **Keep ADR 0006 and make the budgets inputs** (an environment variable, or the query of the setup URL of [ADR 0018](./0018-a-preload-is-a-file-of-the-package-and-its-url-gives-its-settings.md)). The tests get shorter, but the test process still waits for the engine, and the budget still exists in the teardown.
- **Ryuk itself** (`testcontainers/ryuk`). Its session is a TCP port that the client must reach. On an SSH engine, the client reaches only the engine's socket. It also removes no databases inside a server.
- **A reaper held by a TCP port of our own.** Same problem as Ryuk on an SSH engine.
- **A reaper that removes everything, also the local records.** It runs on the engine's host, so it cannot reach the machine of the test process.
- **Derive the teardown budget from the engine call budget.** It keeps both waits.

## Consequences

- No test process and no global teardown waits for the engine to remove the run's objects. A run ends when its last test process ends.
- The engine objects of a killed test process go about 2 s after the last test process of its run ended, also when the whole run is killed, not at the next run. While another test process of the run lives, they stay (item 7).
- Each engine runs one more small container while a run uses it. The image is pulled once on each engine.
- A container with the engine's socket mounted has the rights of the engine on its host. A user who can reach the engine has these rights already.
- `CONTEXT.md` gets the term **Reaper**, and loses **Creating record** when the re-check passes replace it. "Ryuk session" stays an Avoid term.
- The README section "A killed run" changes: the 240 s and the 250 s go, and the reaper's own defaults come in.
- The tests of the reaper run with short values. The two tests of #4205 (251 s and 244 s) go away.
