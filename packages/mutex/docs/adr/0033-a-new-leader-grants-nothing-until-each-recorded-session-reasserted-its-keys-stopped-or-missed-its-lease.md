# A new leader grants nothing until each recorded session reasserted its keys, stopped, or missed its lease

Status: Accepted 2026-11-23 by Ezz (backlog #5265). For the question "#5265 is a real safety defect in @zukhruf/mutex's socket store ... After a failover, the new leader grants nothing for `graceWindow` ms (a timer that starts before it even listens), then refuses any reassert. So a LIVE holder that reconnects late loses its key, and a waiter enters while the holder's callback still runs ... The API change: `graceWindow` goes. The new leader grants nothing a recorded session may hold until that session reasserts (with an end-of-reasserts marker), is dead, or misses its lease (leaseDuration). PROTOCOL_VERSION goes to 3, so old and new processes refuse each other in one directory (ADR 0014).", Ezz chose "Approve; write the ADR, then build (Recommended)". The build comes after this record.

After a failover of the socket lock store, the new leader had a grace window: for `graceWindow` milliseconds (500 by default), it granted no key, so that the holders of the last term could reassert their keys. After the window, it refused each reassert. A refused reassert is a lost lease: the signal of the lease aborts with `LeaseLostError`, and the next waiter gets the key. Thus the callback of the waiter ran while the callback of the holder still ran. Fencing held: the lease of the holder aborted before the grant, and the token of the waiter was higher. The exclusion of the callbacks did not hold.

Two release gates of 2026-11-22 showed it. Under load, one test of the suite "Socket lock server failover" failed in each gate: "The holder must reassert its key on the successor", and "No one may enter while the holder still runs, even when the leader shuts down". Each test passed when it ran alone. Backlog #5265 found the mechanism (evidence in its DIAGNOSED section: the trace loader `trace-hook.mjs`, the runs `q50/` and `slow450/`, and the probes `loop-order.mjs` and `loop-order-linux.mjs`):

- The grace window is a timer, `delay(grace.window)` in the constructor of `LockCoordinator` (`src/lock-stores/remote/lock-coordinator.ts`, lines 65 to 76). `LockServer.start` makes the coordinator before the server listens (`src/lock-stores/socket/lock-server.ts`, lines 54 and 65). Thus the window starts before any holder can reach the new leader.
- The window must be longer than the start of the new lock server, the back-off of the holder (25 ms, × 1.6, at most 1000 ms, `src/lock-stores/socket/electing-connector.ts`, lines 34 to 39), and the time that the operating system gives to both processes. Nothing limits these times. In run 7 of the replica (node:26-alpine with `--cpus=0.5` and four busy loops: 2 of 8 runs failed with the two assertions), the new leader started its window 92 ms before it listened. Its own process sent its reassert 303 ms after the window started, and the new leader refused it. The journal was `['holder:enter', 'waiter:enter']`. With a delay of 450 ms before the successor serves, 3 of 6 runs failed on macOS.
- When a blocked event loop runs again, Node.js runs a timer that expired before it reads a socket that has data (probe `loop-order.mjs`: 10 of 10 runs on macOS and on Linux). Thus the new leader also refuses a reassert that is already in its socket buffer. No value of `graceWindow` removes the race. A larger value makes the race less frequent, and each failover waits longer.
- The tests set `graceWindow` to 200 ms (`src/testing/timing.ts`). That is less than the first five steps of the back-off, approximately 395 ms ([ADR 0031](./0031-a-follower-observes-the-leader-so-a-frozen-leader-is-ended.md)).
- [socket-store.md](../stores/socket-store.md) said that a holder loses its key only when it is frozen for the whole grace window. A live holder that is slow lost its key too.

The lock services that keep locks across a failover do not wait a fixed time (#5265 checked each one). Chubby ([Burrows, OSDI 2006](https://research.google/pubs/the-chubby-lock-service-for-loosely-coupled-distributed-systems/), §2.9): "Session leases are extended to the maximum that the previous master may have been using" (step 3), and "The master waits until each session acknowledges the fail-over event or lets its session expire" (step 6). etcd's lessor renews each lease by its full TTL when a member becomes the leader (`Promote`). Consul renews the TTL of each session after a leader failover. ZooKeeper gives each session its full timeout again. None of them grants the key of an unknown holder after a fixed time.

To wait for each holder, the new leader must know which processes can hold keys. The leader keeps its locks only in its memory, and it records nothing about the processes that it serves. Thus the new leader needs a fact that outlives the old leader. In Chubby, "The database records each session, held lock, and ephemeral file" (§2.9). In ZooKeeper, a new session is a `createSession` request that goes through the whole request chain, and the client gets its answer only at the end of that chain (`ZooKeeperServer.createSession`, `FinalRequestProcessor`).

## Decision

A new leader of a socket lock store recovers each recorded session. It grants no key until each recorded session reasserted its keys and sent the end-of-reasserts marker, stopped, or missed its lease duration. The option `graceWindow` is removed.

### The session record

- Each `SocketStore` object is one **session**. Its id is a random UUID (`randomUUID()` of `node:crypto`), one for each store object. The id is not the process ID. Two stores in one process, or in two worker threads, are two sessions.
- Before the first connection of the store, the session makes its record in two steps:
  1. It takes its presence: a `FileLock` of `@zukhruf/fs` on `<directory>/sessions/<id>.presence`. This is an exclusive SQLite transaction. The kernel ends it when the process or the thread stops, also after `SIGKILL` ([ADR 0012](./0012-a-file-store-holder-is-judged-by-its-presence.md)).
  2. It writes its record, `<directory>/sessions/<id>.json`, with `atomicWrite` of `@zukhruf/fs`: `{"leaseDuration":<milliseconds>,"processId":<pid>}`. The process ID is for a person who reads the directory. No step of the lock store reads it.
- A request goes out only on a connection. Thus each request that a leader can grant comes after the record of its session, and no leader can grant a key to a session that the next leader cannot find. The connector of the store does the two steps once, as `LocalDirectoryConnector` checks the directory once.
- When the store is disposed, after its client closed, the session deletes its record first and frees its presence second. Thus the presence lasts from before the record appears until after the record is gone, as in ADR 0012.
- The record has no list of keys. A list of keys needs a write before each grant (see Considered Options). Thus the record tells only that the session can hold keys.
- The lock server tells if a session lives from its presence, with `FileLock.check`: `locked` is a session that lives, also one that is frozen. `unlocked` or `missing` is a session that stopped or was disposed. A session that stopped never takes its presence again, because its id is new for each store. The lock server never reads a process ID to tell if a session lives. A process ID can name another process after the kernel uses it again, and a zombie process answers `kill(pid, 0)` (ADR 0012, [election ADR 0006](../../../election/docs/adr/0006-a-candidate-ends-a-leader-that-is-silent-past-the-lease-duration.md)). The lock server does not check the presence of its own session: it lives.

What occurs when a process stops or freezes at each step:

| Step of the session                                              | The process stops                                                                                                                   | The process freezes                                                                                                   |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| It took its presence, and has no record yet                      | Nothing is held. The next recovery deletes the presence file, because it has no record and no lock.                                 | Nothing is held, and no recovery waits for it: a leader reads only the records.                                       |
| It has its record, and holds no key                              | The next recovery finds its presence free, and deletes the record and the presence file.                                            | The next recovery waits for it until its lease duration ends. Then the recovery of that session ends.                 |
| It holds a key, and the leader runs                              | The leader releases its keys when the kernel closes its connection, as before. The record stays until the next recovery deletes it. | It keeps its keys, as before: a leader that runs never takes a key from a connection that is open.                    |
| It holds a key, and the leader stops                             | The new leader finds its presence free at the listing, or at a later check. Its recovery ends at once, and its keys are free.       | The new leader waits until the lease duration of the session ends, then refuses its reasserts. The session is fenced. |
| It is disposed: the record is deleted, and the presence is freed | Between the two steps: the next recovery deletes the presence file, because it has no record and no lock.                           | Between the two steps: no record, so no recovery waits for it.                                                        |

A leader that stops or freezes during its recovery is replaced as each leader is ([ADR 0031](./0031-a-follower-observes-the-leader-so-a-frozen-leader-is-ended.md)). The next leader reads the same records, and each session reasserts again.

### Recovery

**Recovery** replaces the grace window. The word is the one of PostgreSQL, which ADR 0018 already uses: after a crash, the next start does crash recovery.

- **When it starts.** A lock server recovers when its term is not the first term of the directory, and the term before it did not end with a clean shutdown (`term.afterCleanShutdown`, [ADR 0018](./0018-the-next-leader-skips-the-grace-window-after-a-clean-shutdown.md)). After it won its term, and before it listens, the lock server lists `sessions/` and checks the presence of each record. It deletes the record and the presence file of each session that stopped, and each presence file that has no record and no lock. The other recorded sessions, its own session too, are the recovering sessions. Then it listens.
- **The deadline of a session.** It is the `leaseDuration` in the record of the session, counted on the clock of the store from the listen. The listen comes after the failover, so each session gets at least its lease duration after the failover. Chubby extends each session lease to the longest that the last master could have given (§2.9, step 3) for the same reason.
- **What it grants meanwhile.** Nothing. An acquire waits, a `try` is answered `busy`, and a holder check waits for the end of the recovery, as in the grace window. The coordinator takes each reassert of a recovering session. For two claims on one key, the newer token wins, as before. Chubby's master also waits until each session acknowledged the failover or expired (§2.9, step 6).
- **The keys of the leader's own process.** The client of the leader reaches its own lock server over the socket after the listen, and it reasserts as each other session does. It needs no step before the listen: no key is granted before each recovering session ended, so the order of the reasserts does not change a result.
- **What ends the recovery of one session.** The first of these three facts:
  1. Its marker. After its `hello`, the client of a session sends a `reassert` for each key that it holds, then the end-of-reasserts marker `{"op":"reasserted"}` on the same connection. A session that holds no key sends only the marker. A socket keeps the order of the lines, so the marker comes after each reassert of the session.
  2. It stopped: its presence is free. The kernel tells nobody when a presence frees, so the lock server checks again. It checks when a connection of the session closes before its marker, and at each step of the back-off of ADR 0031 (25 ms, × 1.6, ± 20 %, at most 1000 ms, `Backoff` of `@zukhruf/async`) on the clock of the store. It then deletes the record and the presence file. A check changes only when the recovery ends, never which key a holder gets.
  3. Its deadline came: the session missed its lease. The session can still run, for example after `SIGCONT`. Thus the lock server keeps its record.
- **The end of the recovery.** When each recovering session ended, the coordinator grants the waiters in the order of their requests, as it did after the grace window.
- **A late reassert.** The coordinator answers `rejected` to a reassert that comes after the recovery, to a reassert of a session whose deadline came, and to a reassert of a session that was not in the listing. A session that made its record after the listing never sent a request to an earlier term, so it holds no key of an earlier term. The client reads `rejected` as a lost lease: the signal of the lease aborts with `LeaseLostError`, and the call rejects with `LeaseLostError` ([ADR 0013](./0013-a-lost-lease-aborts-the-signal-of-the-lease.md)). The token of that lease is lower than each token of the new term, so a fenced resource refuses its late writes ([ADR 0003](./0003-a-fencing-token-on-every-lease.md)).
- **A clean shutdown.** The rule of ADR 0018 stays: a lock server resigns clean when no key is held and its recovery is over or it had none (`LockCoordinator.leavesNothingToRecover`). The next term then has no recovery, also while live sessions have records: no key was held, so no session has a key to reassert.

### The protocol

- `PROTOCOL_VERSION` becomes 3 ([ADR 0014](./0014-a-process-says-its-protocol-version-before-its-first-request.md)).
- The `hello` carries the id of the session: `{"op":"hello","version":3,"session":"<id>"}`. `op` and `version` keep their shape. Thus a leader of protocol version 2 reads it as a `hello` of another version and refuses it, and the process fails its acquire with `ProtocolVersionError`. A leader of protocol version 3 refuses a `hello` of version 2 in the same way. It also refuses a `hello` of version 3 with no `session`, as it refuses each first line that is not a `hello` of its protocol version. The `welcome` and the `refused` answer do not change.
- The marker is a new request, `{"op":"reasserted"}`. It has no `id`, and the coordinator does not answer it.
- An IPC channel and a worker port have no `hello` and no failover ([ADR 0004](./0004-parent-stops-held-keys-stay.md)). Their client is `RemoteLockClient` too, so it also sends the marker after it connects. Their coordinator has no recovery, so it ignores the marker. They need no session id and no session record.

### The API

- `SocketStoreOptions.graceWindow` is removed, with no alias.
- `leaseDuration` now gives the longest silence of this process that the others accept in two cases: while it leads ([ADR 0031](./0031-a-follower-observes-the-leader-so-a-frozen-leader-is-ended.md)), and during the recovery after a failover. Thus one number gives the longest pause of the process. The default stays 15000 ms, the default of `SqliteElection`.
- `clock` also runs the deadlines and the checks of the recovery.
- The recovery ends no later than the longest lease duration of a recovering session that still runs and is silent. A session that stopped ends its recovery at the next check. A session that runs ends it as soon as it reconnects and sends its marker.

## Considered Options

- **A grace window of a fixed time.** This was the behavior before. It refuses a live holder that reconnects after the window, and nothing limits that time. The probe `loop-order.mjs` shows that the window can end before a reassert that already arrived is read. A larger value only makes the race less frequent, and it makes each failover wait longer.
- **A grace window that starts after the listen, or one that a leader renews.** It is still a timer. A holder can be slower than each value of it, and the order of the event loop stays the same. Chubby calls its lock-delay, a timer, an imperfect protection (§2.4, as single-flight ADR 0010 cites it). The single flight removed its grace window for this reason ([single-flight ADR 0010](../../../single-flight/docs/adr/0010-a-leader-holds-a-kernel-lock-on-its-key-until-its-outcome-is-handed-over.md)).
- **The leader records each grant in the directory before it answers (write-ahead).** This is how Chubby and ZooKeeper keep their state. It leaves no gap: a grant that is not on disk was never answered. But each grant and each release waits for a file write on the leader, on the path that the socket store makes fast. A record that the leader writes also cannot tell a frozen holder from a holder that stopped. Only a kernel lock of the holder's own process can tell it, so each holder makes a file anyway. When the leader dies or freezes between the write and the answer, the record names a grant that its session never got, and that session reasserts nothing for it, so the record is safe but stale. Each release must delete or rewrite the record, or the records grow.
- **Each holder records itself before its first acquire, and deletes its record when it holds no key and waits for none.** Only the sessions that can hold keys have a record, so an idle frozen process does not delay a recovery. But each acquire after an idle time takes a presence and writes a record before its request goes out, and its last release deletes both: the cost of a file lock store on each acquire. The delete must also wait for each request on its way, or a grant can arrive after the record is gone.
- **The set of sessions with an open store, from before the first connection until the store is disposed (membership).** This option was selected. It costs one presence for the life of the store, one write before the first connection and two deletes at the disposal, and nothing for each grant. It leaves no gap: the record exists before each request. Its cost: a frozen process that holds no key delays a recovery up to its lease duration. Chubby waits for each session in the same way, also for a session that holds no lock.
- **A kernel lock on each key, held by the holder (single-flight ADR 0010).** A frozen holder keeps its key with no limit, and a recovery never waits for another key. But Ezz's decision ends the claim of a frozen holder at its lease duration and fences it, as each lease of the mutex can be lost ([ADR 0003](./0003-a-fencing-token-on-every-lease.md), [ADR 0013](./0013-a-lost-lease-aborts-the-signal-of-the-lease.md)). A kernel lock on each key for each acquire is also the design of `SqliteStore`. A caller that wants a frozen holder to keep its key with no limit can use that lock store.
- **A list of keys in the record, so that a recovery grants each key that no recovering session can hold.** A key that a holder gets must be in the list before its grant reaches the holder. Thus the list needs a write before each grant, by the leader or by the holder. Without it, the new leader can grant a key that a live holder still reasserts later.
- **The process ID to tell if a session lives (`kill(pid, 0)`).** ADR 0012 found it wrong for containers, worker threads and zombie processes. Election ADR 0006 rejected it as a check that a used-again process ID answers incorrectly.
- **The connection to tell if a session lives.** A session that did not reconnect yet has no connection. A frozen session cannot accept one, and a full listen queue looks like a process that stopped (ADR 0012).
- **One `recover` message with each held key, in place of the reasserts and the marker.** A list of reasserts with no end could not occur. But Ezz's words name the reasserts and a marker, and with the marker the `reassert` request and its `rejected` answer stay as they are.
- **The keys of the leader's own process go into its coordinator before the listen.** Nothing is granted before each recovering session ended, so the order does not change a result. A path from the leader to its own coordinator without the socket was rejected in ADR 0018.

## Consequences

- A live holder keeps its key across a failover, also when it reconnects slowly, while it reconnects within its lease duration. A waiter enters only after the release. The new leader no longer refuses the reassert of its own process.
- A holder that is frozen for longer than its lease duration, counted from the listen of the new leader, loses its key. When it runs again, the signal of its lease aborts with `LeaseLostError`, and a fenced resource refuses its writes.
- A process that is frozen or blocked during a failover delays each grant of the directory, up to its lease duration, also when it holds no key. Set `leaseDuration` above the longest pause of your processes, as ADR 0031 says. A shorter lease ends a frozen leader sooner, and it ends a recovery sooner.
- A process that stopped delays a recovery by at most one step of the back-off after the listing.
- The directory has a folder `sessions` with two files for each live store. A process that stopped leaves its two files until the next recovery. Do not delete a file in `sessions` while processes use the directory: a new leader would not wait for that session, and it would grant the keys of that session.
- Each store keeps one SQLite connection open for its life, for its presence.
- Processes of protocol versions 2 and 3 refuse each other with `ProtocolVersionError`. A holder of protocol version 2 has no record, so a leader of protocol version 3 cannot wait for it. Use one package version in all processes of a directory, as before.
- Code of the build: `remote/lock-coordinator.ts` (`GraceWindow` becomes a recovery phase that the facts end), `socket/lock-server.ts` (the listing before the listen, the deadlines and the checks), `remote/remote-lock-client.ts` (`#resume` sends the marker), `remote/protocol.ts` and `socket/handshake.ts` (the marker, the session id and `PROTOCOL_VERSION` 3), `socket/socket-store.ts` (the session record, and no `graceWindow`), the comments of `socket/electing-connector.ts` that name the grace window, and `src/testing/timing.ts` (no `graceWindow`).
- `@zukhruf/single-flight` copies the handshake, the lock server, the electing connector and the failover rules of the client ([copied-from-mutex.md](../../../single-flight/docs/copied-from-mutex.md)). It has had no grace window since its ADR 0010, so it has no copy of the defect. The build records the session id, the marker and the recovery as differences in that ledger, and runs `npx nx run copy-check:check`.
- [failure-modes.md](../concepts/failure-modes.md), [fencing-tokens.md](../concepts/fencing-tokens.md) and [acquire-modes.md](../concepts/acquire-modes.md) still name the grace window. The build updates them.

The tests that prove the decision. Each test must fail before the build, or fail when a mutation removes the rule that it names:

1. `src/lock-stores/remote/remote-locking.test.ts`, with a fake clock: "a holder that reasserts long after the failover, within the lease duration of its session, keeps its key, and a waiter is granted only after the release". The clock moves 5000 ms before the reassert, with a lease duration of 15000 ms. Before the build, the reassert is refused.
2. Same file: "a new leader grants nothing until each recorded session sent its marker". Two recorded sessions; one reasserts a key and sends its marker; an acquire of another key waits; the second session sends only its marker; then the acquire is granted. This also shows that an empty list of reasserts ends a recovery.
3. Same file: "a session that misses its lease duration is fenced". The clock moves past the lease duration of a silent session. The recovery ends, a waiter gets the key with a higher token, and a later reassert of that session is answered `rejected`.
4. Same file: "a reassert of a session that the listing did not find is rejected".
5. Same file: "a child of an IPC coordinator that sends the marker keeps its keys and gets its answers as before".
6. `src/lock-stores/socket/socket-store.test.ts`: "a store records its session before its first request and deletes the record and then the presence when it is disposed". The test reads `sessions/` at each step.
7. Same file: "a recorded session whose process was killed ends its recovery at once, and the new leader deletes its files". The killed process has a lease duration of 60000 ms, so only the check of the presence can end its recovery within the time of the test.
8. Same file: "a frozen process that holds no key delays a grant after a failover until its lease duration ends, and no longer". The frozen process has a lease duration of 500 ms.
9. Same file: "a lone process after a clean shutdown acquires at once, also while a recorded process is frozen". This keeps ADR 0018 true with the records.
10. `src/lock-stores/socket/handshake.test.ts`: a leader of protocol version 3 refuses a `hello` of version 2 and a `hello` of version 3 with no `session`. A process of protocol version 3 fails with `ProtocolVersionError` against a leader of version 2. `src/lock-stores/socket/wire-compatibility.test.ts` spells out the new `hello`, the marker and the `sessions` files, so a later change to one of them fails there.
11. The suite "Socket lock server failover" in `src/lock-stores/socket/socket-store.test.ts` waits for facts, not for time:
    - "a leader that shuts down while a follower holds a key still lets a waiter enter only after the holder releases" and "a holder keeps its key when the leader dies, and a waiter enters only after it releases": `await delay(graceWindow * 2)` is removed. After the holder and the waiter reached the next leader, the waiter asks `isHeld('product:42')`, which answers only after the recovery, and reports `true`. Then it makes one try with `Modes.skipIfBusy()` and reports that it did not acquire the key. Only then does the test send `finish`. The journal must be `['holder:enter', 'holder:leave', 'waiter:enter']`, and the outcome of the holder must be `'ok'`.
    - "a leader frozen past its lease duration is ended by a follower, a survivor leads, and the holder keeps its key": no change of the test. Its `isHeld` now answers after the recovery, which waits for the reassert of the holder, so it no longer depends on the load of the computer.
    - "a holder frozen past the grace window is fenced off and learns its lock was lost" becomes "a holder frozen past its lease duration is fenced off and learns its lock was lost". The frozen holder has a lease duration of 500 ms. `await delay(graceWindow)` is removed: after `SIGCONT`, the body of the thawed holder waits for the `abort` event of its lease signal, and then it writes. The write must be `'stale'`, and the outcome must be `'LeaseLostError'`.
    - The tests in `socket-store-coordinator.test.ts` and `remote-locking.test.ts` that name the grace window are replaced by the tests 1 to 4.
